The site

A relief valve for the sewers of Brynmawr

Brynmawr Blackrock is a combined sewer system: household waste and rainwater run through the same pipes to Welsh Water's treatment works above the A465. In dry weather everything is treated. In wet weather, when the pipes can't carry the volume, a storm overflow is permitted to open and discharge diluted, untreated sewage directly into the nearest watercourse — here, the River Clydach — rather than let it back up into homes and streets.

That's the design intent. What the Event Duration Monitor at this outfall has recorded since 1 January 2026 is how often the valve actually opens, and for how long. This page reads that monitor's full history for asset DCWW438 against the calendar.

Why it matters

A small river, asked to absorb a lot

The Clydach at Blackrock is a headwater tributary — narrow, fast-flowing, and with little volume to dilute what's added to it. A discharge that would be barely noticeable in a large lowland river can dominate a stream this size, depressing oxygen levels, smothering gravel beds that fish and invertebrates depend on, and pushing bacteria counts up for anyone wading, walking a dog, or letting children play downstream.

Event Duration Monitors like this one don't record volume or what's actually in the discharge — only when the overflow opens and closes. That makes them a measure of frequency and duration, not severity. Welsh Water's own storm overflow reduction targets, set under the Environment Agency's and Natural Resources Wales's frameworks, are judged against exactly this kind of record — which is why independent, public access to it, via Surfers Against Sewage's Live Sewage Map, matters.
